Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Lawton

Many mid-season breakdowns start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Change your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of the year.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.

Common HVAC Problems in Lawton

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of problems. Hard-running summers push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and humidity and airborne grit slowly foul outdoor condenser coils, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Clogged filters quietly choke off airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, damage that builds slowly and out of sight.

We also see the full range of component failures in local homes: capacitors that leave the outdoor unit humming but not starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, slow refrigerant leaks that leave the house lukewarm by afternoon, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. The area's older housing stock adds its own layer, undersized returns, leaky or crushed ductwork, and additions that no duct run ever reached.

Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are far cheaper to address early.
