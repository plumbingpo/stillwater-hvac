Think of a heat pump as an air conditioner that can work in both directions. In summer it pulls heat out of your home; in winter a reversing valve flips the refrigerant flow, and the same compressor draws heat from the outdoor air and brings it inside. Because it moves heat instead of creating it, a heat pump can deliver two to three times more heat energy than the electricity it uses, which is a big reason homeowners in Stillwater, OK are making the switch as older systems wear out. Today's cold-climate models, built around variable-speed inverter compressors, keep producing useful heat well below freezing, far beyond the heat pumps of twenty years ago.

Great heat pump performance starts with design work, and that is where Stillwater HVAC earns your trust. A heat pump has to be sized for two loads, summer cooling and winter heating, and the design has to find the balance point where the outdoor temperature meets the unit's capacity curve. Our experienced technicians run the load calculation, compare single-stage, two-stage, and variable-speed inverter equipment against your climate and ductwork, and plan the backup heat properly, either electric strips staged below the balance point or a dual-fuel setup with a gas furnace that takes over in deep cold. We also check your electrical service and ducts, since heat pumps deliver lower supply temperatures than furnaces and need solid airflow to feel comfortable.

Installation follows the same discipline as every Stillwater HVAC system: line set pressure-tested and evacuated to a micron-verified vacuum, charge weighed in and confirmed with superheat and subcooling, the outdoor unit elevated on a stand where winter defrost drainage demands it, condensate handled properly, and the thermostat configured so staging, backup lockouts, and defrost behave the way the engineering intended. Then we commission in both modes, heating and cooling, and record the readings. How to Prevent Heat Pump Installation Problems

Smart maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow drifts, leaves, and shrubs year-round
  • Keep temperature settings steady so the backup strips are not needed to catch up
  • Change filters on schedule; low airflow hurts a heat pump in both seasons
  • Gently hose off the outdoor coil every spring before cooling season
  • Book twice-yearly maintenance so charge and defrost stay verified in both modes
  • Light frost and an occasional defrost hiss are normal in winter, but solid ice calls for service
  • Check your first winter bill, since an unexpected jump often points to overworked backup heat
